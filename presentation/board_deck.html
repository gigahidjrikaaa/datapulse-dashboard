Executive summary

Sales are growing; profit is leaking through discounting and freight

One key message for the Board
Global Superstore does not have a growth problem. It has a margin-leakage problem: about a quarter of all order lines sell at a loss, and the loss is concentrated in deep discounts and loss-making overseas territories. Tightening price control and fixing those territories recovers roughly $1.23M of operating profit without adding a single new sale.

Diagnosis · Margin

Operating margin stayed near 11% while sales climbed 90%

Gross sales and operating margin$ million (bars) · margin %, FY2011-FY2014
Gross sales (bars)Operating margin (line)
Volume was rewarded, margin was not
  • Revenue compounded at 23.9% a year, but operating margin moved only from 11.0% to 11.7%.
  • Profit grew almost exactly in line with revenue, so every extra dollar of sales carried the same thin margin.
  • The company measures and pays for gross volume, so sales teams had no reason to defend price.
  • Margin, not revenue, is the number the Board should track from here.
Source: Global Superstore transaction ledger, FY2011-FY2014. Operating margin = profit / gross sales.

Diagnosis · Geography

Losses concentrate in four countries, led by Turkey and Nigeria

Operating profit by country, ten largest deficits$ thousand, FY2011-FY2014 cumulative
A company-policy loss, not a market loss
  • Turkey (-$98K) and Nigeria (-$81K) alone account for $179K of the deficit.
  • 29 of 147 countries are net-negative; the top four carry 56% of that deficit.
  • Both were sold at extreme average discounts: 60% in Turkey and 70% in Nigeria.
  • The EMEA market (Europe, Middle East and Africa) runs a 5.4% margin, well below 12-13% in the other markets.
  • No discount or freight cost was charged back to these territories, so their losses were absorbed centrally.
Source: Global Superstore transaction ledger, FY2011-FY2014. Discount is the order-line average; country profit is net of all lines in that country.

Diagnosis · Discounting

Order economics invert once the discount passes 20%

Operating margin by discount bandmargin %, all order lines FY2011-FY2014
Profitable margin (≤20% discount)Operating deficit (>20% discount)Dashed line marks break-even threshold
The 20% break-even
  • Below 20% every line is profitable: margin runs from +25.3% at no discount to +9.9% at 10-20%.
  • Above 20% every band is loss-making, sliding from -5.5% to -111%.
  • 11,328 lines (22.1%) were priced above the 20% line and lost $814,682 in total.
  • Gross margin cannot cover concessions deeper than 20%, so the cap follows from the data.
Source: Global Superstore transaction ledger, FY2011-FY2014. Bands follow the discount distribution in the ledger.

Turnaround · Roadmap

Execution runs from immediate price controls to a permanent margin committee

Four-phase implementationmonths 1-12
1
Commercial control
Lock a 20% discount ceiling into order entry and move sales pay from volume to margin contribution.
Months 1-2 · Target: no order above 20% without approval
2
Territory restructuring
Move Turkey and Nigeria to local master distributors and bonded third-party logistics, ending cross-border air freight.
Months 3-4 · Target: regional loss cut 70%
3
Freight and Tables repricing
Add dimensional freight surcharges on bulky lines and reprice the Tables portfolio.
Months 5-7 · Target: Tables at break-even
4
Institutional review
Run quarterly margin audits across all territories and stand up a permanent margin committee.
Months 8-12 · Target: margin above 15.5%
Source: turnaround plan derived from the FY2011-FY2014 diagnosis. Phase 1 begins on Board approval.

Board decision

The Board must approve the 20% discount cap and 3PL restructuring this quarter

Three decisions requested
  • Approve the 20% discount cap in order entry, with VP sign-off for discounts between 15% and 20% and a hard block above 20%.
  • Authorise the 3PL transition for Turkey and Nigeria to remove the cross-border loss.
  • Charter a margin committee chaired by the CFO to review discount leakage and Tables margin every quarter.
Decision today, first savings inside 90 days
Phase 1 needs no new capital and no new systems beyond an order-entry rule.
